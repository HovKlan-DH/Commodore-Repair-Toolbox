How the mockups fit together. All of them keep the left panel, thumbnails and overlay panels untouched, and all store jobs locally in a per-job folder under the data root.

Every entry carries two independent fields. Category says what it is — Note (documentation, no fault), Cosmetic (flux, scratches, old repair marks, no electrical meaning), Suspected (might be a problem, needs investigation), Confirmed (established by measurement or observation). State says where it stands: Pending until it becomes Fixed or Ruled out — the third one matters, because a suspicion you tested and cleared is neither pending nor fixed. On the board the pin is coloured by category and gets a green check when fixed, so one look answers both questions at once. Replacing a part is work, not a status: it belongs in the description and the part-used field.

The board's identity is a free-text Board ID, not a serial number — a serial if the machine has one, your own label if it does not, or empty.

A; Create entry. The active job sits in a full-width strip above the tab row, visible from every tab, so the capture always knows where the entry goes. Hold the mouse and drag a box of any size on the board; on release the quick card opens under it, already anchored to that rectangle. Right-clicking a component instead opens the same card anchored to C36 rather than to an area.

The quick card is deliberately small: category, state, one line of text, and a tick-list of the components that fall inside the box you drew — so the entry knows its scope and a job can later be summarised by component. Photos and everything else live in B; Full entry details — a separate window like the component popup, with description, part used, time and cost, a measurements table, captioned photos, oscilloscope captures, files and the anchor itself (re-draw the area, switch side, attach related components). Any saved entry reopens in that editor from the Worklog tab, so nothing typed in the quick card is a dead end.

C; Worklog tab: jobs for the selected board on the left, and the board itself at the centre of the selected job. Entries are numbered pins on the board and numbered cards in the list beside it, so a click on either side finds the other. Serial number belongs to the board, so #0042 and #0031 share ID 249381.
